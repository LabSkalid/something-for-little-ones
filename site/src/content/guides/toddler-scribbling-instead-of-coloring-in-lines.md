You hand your two-year-old a coloring page, and they cover the whole sheet, including the picture, the margins, and sometimes the table. It can look like they are ignoring the page.

They are not. Scribbling is how most children start. Coloring inside the lines comes later, and it comes at different times for different children.

## What scribbling is

At this age, a child is learning how their hand and arm move and what a crayon does on paper. Big back-and-forth marks, circles, and dots are all part of this. The point is not the picture. The point is making a mark and seeing it appear.

Many children also change in small steps. Marks become more controlled, then children begin to choose colors, and later they begin to care about the shape on the page. Each child moves at their own pace, and a sibling or a friend of the same age may be in a different place.

## What helps

**Use big, simple pages.** A page with one huge shape and thick lines lets a child feel successful even when the marks go everywhere. Pages with small details can feel frustrating before a child is ready. The [Big Shape coloring pages](/big-shape-coloring-pages/) and [First Animal coloring pages](/first-animal-coloring-pages/) are made for this stage, with big areas and nothing tiny to stay inside.

**Keep sessions short.** A page that is done in a minute or two is normal. Offer another one if your child wants it, and do not insist if they walk away.

**Offer chunky tools.** Large crayons are easier to hold than thin pencils. Washable options help you relax about the table.

**Color alongside your child.** Children copy what they see. Color your own page next to theirs, and talk about colors without correcting their work.

**Praise the effort, not the result.** "You used so much blue!" works better than "stay inside the lines."

**Do not fix their page.** Drawing over the marks or showing "the right way" can teach a child that their version is wrong. Let it stay theirs.

## When the lines start to matter

Many children begin to notice the shape, and they may start to slow down on their own. When that happens, move up gently to pages with a few more parts, such as the [animal coloring pages](/animal-coloring-pages/), [dinosaur coloring pages](/dinosaur-coloring-pages/), or [car coloring pages](/car-coloring-pages/) for ages 3 to 4.

A simple way to tell if a page is the right level: if your child stays with it for a few minutes and wants to talk about it, it fits. If they give up right away, try a simpler one. For more detail, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).

## A quick note

Children develop at different speeds, and a wide range is normal. If you have worries about your child's hand use, drawing, or development in general, a pediatrician is the right person to ask. This page is only meant to help you choose coloring activities at home.
