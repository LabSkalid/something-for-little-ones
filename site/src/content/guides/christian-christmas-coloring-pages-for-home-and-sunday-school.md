For many families, Christmas is first of all the story of Jesus' birth. Coloring gives children a calm way to spend time with that story, whether you are at the kitchen table, in a Sunday school room, or at a family gathering at church.

Here are practical ways to use the [Christian Christmas coloring pages](/christian-christmas-coloring-pages/) with children of different ages.

## 1. Color while you tell the story

Read or tell the Christmas story from the Gospels in your own words, and let children color while they listen. Many children listen more attentively when their hands are busy. Stop now and then and ask, "What do you think happened next?" so the coloring and the story go together.

## 2. Use one page for each part of the season

If you read a little each evening before Christmas, give each reading its own page. Children can color the picture, and you can talk about what you read. For a daily plan that works with these pages, see [Christmas countdown with coloring pages](/guides/christmas-countdown-with-coloring-pages/).

## 3. Memory work

Write a short verse or a thankful sentence on the back of the page, or at the bottom with a pencil, and let older children copy it in their own handwriting. Younger children can say it out loud while they color. The [Glory to God page](/christian-christmas-coloring-pages/glory-to-god/) is a good place to start.

## 4. A take-home sheet for parents

In Sunday school, a colored page is something children can carry home and show their family. Add a line on the back with the day's story or question, such as "Ask me about the story we heard today." It gives parents an easy way to start the conversation.

## 5. Class display

Hang finished pages on a string, a bulletin board, or a wall in the hallway. Children enjoy seeing their work on display, and families notice it when they come in.

## 6. Cards for church friends and neighbors

Fold a sheet of paper in half and tape a colored page to the front. Children can write a name or a short message, and the card can go to a neighbor, a grandparent, or someone who may be alone over the holidays.

## 7. Early finishers and arrival time

In a Sunday school room, some children arrive early and others finish first. A tray of pages and crayons near the door gives them something calm to do, and it lets the teacher greet each family without interruptions.

## Choose the right level

Children of different ages sit together in many Sunday school classes and at home. The pages are split by age, so you can set out two or three levels and let each child choose. A page that is too hard ends in frustration, and one that is too easy is done in a minute. See [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/) for help.

## Mixing with other Christmas pages

Some families like to use both the Christian Christmas pages and the general [Christmas coloring pages](/christmas-coloring-pages/), with Santa, reindeer, and the tree. They can sit side by side in the same pile, and children can choose. For more ways to use them at home, see [Christmas coloring activities for kids at home](/guides/christmas-coloring-activities-for-kids-at-home/).
