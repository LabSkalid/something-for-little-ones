Thanksgiving day has a long stretch where the adults are busy and the children are waiting. A pile of printed coloring pages and a cup of crayons fills that stretch better than almost anything else, and it needs no preparation on the day itself.

Here are a few ways to use [free printable Thanksgiving coloring pages](/thanksgiving-coloring-pages/) beyond handing out a sheet and hoping for the best.

## 1. Set up the kids' table before anyone arrives

Print three or four different pages, with a few copies of each, so nobody has to fight over the one turkey. Give every child a cup of crayons so the colors are shared evenly. If you use markers, choose washable ones, and keep the tablecloth in mind.

Print the day before. A printer that runs out of ink on Thursday morning is a very common problem. If you need help with settings, see [how to print](/how-to-print/).

## 2. Use the hour before dinner

When guests are arriving and the kitchen is busy, children often run out of things to do. A coloring station near the table works as a quiet place to settle. Put the pages out an hour before dinner, and let the children come and go as they like.

## 3. Make thankful feathers

If you choose a turkey page with a big fan of tail feathers, ask each child to color a feather and say one thing they are thankful for. Older children can write it on the feather. Younger children can tell you, and you write it down.

The finished turkey goes on the fridge or the dinner table, and it can stay up for the rest of the week.

## 4. Turn pages into place cards

Fold a finished page in half, write the guest's name on the front, and set it at their plate. Grandparents tend to keep these. For a younger child, pick a page with big open areas, so nothing needs fine detail.

## 5. Mixed ages at one table

When children are five and seven, or three and eight, one page rarely suits everyone. The Thanksgiving pages are split by age, so you can put a simple page with big areas next to a fuller scene and let each child choose. If you are not sure which level fits your child, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).

## 6. A folder for the trip

If you are traveling to a relative's house, put a few printed pages, a pencil case of crayons, and a clipboard or a hard book into a folder. It is easier on a car seat or a plane tray than a tablet, and nothing needs charging.

## A short checklist

- Print the day before, and make a few extra copies.
- Choose pages for two or three age levels.
- Crayons in cups, one cup per child.
- Add a pencil for writing names and thankful words.
- Keep a few pages in reserve for children who finish early.
