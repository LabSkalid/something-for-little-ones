Every class has a few children who finish early and then have nothing to do. Without a plan, "I'm done" turns into wandering, chatting, and interruptions for the children who are still working.

Printable coloring pages are an easy answer. They need no explanation, no supplies beyond crayons, and no marking. Here is how teachers use [free printable coloring pages](/) in the classroom.

## Build an early finisher bin

Fill a tray or a folder near the door with printed pages and a cup of crayons or colored pencils. When a child finishes, they know where to go without asking.

A few things make the bin work better:

- **Several levels.** Include pages for different levels so every child finds something suitable. The sets are organized by age, from [big shapes](/big-shape-coloring-pages/) for the youngest to [town scenes](/town-scene-coloring-pages/) and [woodland animals](/woodland-animal-coloring-pages/) for ages 6 to 8.
- **A few themes.** Choose two or three themes that match your current unit, such as [dinosaurs](/dinosaur-coloring-pages/), [ocean scenes](/ocean-scene-coloring-pages/), or [rockets](/rocket-coloring-pages/).
- **A "done" place.** One tray for finished pages keeps desks clear and gives children somewhere to put their work.

## Morning work

Coloring is a calm way to start the day while you take attendance and settle the room. Put a page on each desk before children arrive, or let them take one from a tray as they come in. Children who arrive late can join in without missing instructions.

## Transitions and quiet moments

Between lessons, before lunch, or after recess, a short coloring activity helps a room settle. Keep the pages simple so the activity can be started and stopped easily, and let children keep working on the same page across the day.

## A substitute folder

A sub folder with printed coloring pages is easy to prepare. It gives a substitute teacher something that needs no instructions, and it works for any grade you teach. Add a short note on which sets suit which age.

## Seasonal sets for parties and special weeks

When the schedule changes, holiday pages fill the gaps. The site has sets for [Fall](/fall-coloring-pages/), [Halloween](/halloween-coloring-pages/), [Thanksgiving](/thanksgiving-coloring-pages/), [Christmas](/christmas-coloring-pages/), [Christian Christmas](/christian-christmas-coloring-pages/), and [Winter](/winter-coloring-pages/), and each can be opened by age.

## Mixed levels in one class

In one classroom, children's skills can vary a lot. Instead of giving everyone the same page, set out two or three levels and let children choose. Choosing keeps the page interesting for the child, and nobody feels labeled. For help choosing levels, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).
