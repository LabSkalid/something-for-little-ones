A snow day, a long school break, or a week at home with a cold: some days arrive without a plan. A stack of printed coloring pages and a cup of crayons needs no preparation on the day, no screen, and almost no cleanup.

Here is how to get ready for those days, with ideas for different ages.

## Make a winter break box

Fill a shallow box or a folder with:

- 10 to 15 printed coloring pages from different themes
- crayons or colored pencils in a cup
- a clipboard or a hard book to lean on

Keep it on a shelf where your child can reach it, and bring it out when the day needs a calm hour. Print the pages ahead of time, because a printer that fails on a snowy morning is a very common problem. See [how to print](/how-to-print/) for settings.

## Choose a few winter pages

The [Winter coloring pages](/winter-coloring-pages/) are split by age, so you can match the level to your child. Add a few from the [Christmas set](/christmas-coloring-pages/) if the holiday is close, and a few that have nothing to do with winter, such as [dinosaurs](/dinosaur-coloring-pages/) or [rockets](/rocket-coloring-pages/), for children who are tired of snowmen.

## Match the page to your child

- **Ages 2–3:** big shapes, done in a few minutes. See [Big Shape coloring pages](/big-shape-coloring-pages/) and [First Animal coloring pages](/first-animal-coloring-pages/).
- **Ages 3–4:** animals, dinosaurs, and cars with bold outlines.
- **Ages 4–5:** [garden](/garden-coloring-pages/) and [simple vehicle](/simple-vehicle-coloring-pages/) pages with more parts.
- **Ages 5–6:** [ocean](/ocean-scene-coloring-pages/) and [dinosaur](/dinosaur-scene-coloring-pages/) scenes that take a longer sitting.
- **Ages 6–8:** [town](/town-scene-coloring-pages/) and [woodland animal](/woodland-animal-coloring-pages/) pages.

If you are not sure where your child fits, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).

## Ideas for different days

**Snow days.** Start the morning with a short coloring session before going outside, and come back to it when mittens are wet and everyone needs to warm up. Hot chocolate and a coloring page make a good pair.

**Long break weeks.** Plan one coloring hour a day, at the same time. A predictable quiet hour helps the rest of the day go more smoothly. If the break includes Christmas, a [coloring countdown](/guides/christmas-countdown-with-coloring-pages/) fills the days before.

**Sick days.** When your child is home and tired, a simple page and a few crayons on a tray are easy to manage from the couch. Keep the pages simple and the tools within reach.

**Travel to relatives.** A folder with a few pages and a pencil case is easier to carry than a bag of toys, and nothing needs charging. Crayons are safer than markers in a moving car.

**Mixed ages.** Print a page from a different level for each child and let them color at the same table, so nobody feels given the "baby" page.

## Make it a little more interesting

- **A story page.** Ask, "Where is this going next?" and let your child tell it while coloring.
- **A color challenge.** "Can you use only cold colors?" turns a snowy page into a game.
- **A gallery.** Hang finished pages on a string with clothespins and add to it through the break.
- **A gift.** Color a page for a grandparent or a neighbor.
