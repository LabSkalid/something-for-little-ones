Children count the days to Christmas, and an advent calendar helps them do it. A coloring countdown does the same job with paper and crayons: one page a day, and a short quiet moment you can count on.

It takes about ten minutes to set up, and you can start on any day in December.

## What you need

- A stack of printed [Christmas coloring pages](/christmas-coloring-pages/)
- Crayons or colored pencils
- Envelopes, paper bags, or a folder with numbers on it
- Tape or a string with clothespins, if you want a display

## Set it up in four steps

1. **Pick the number of days.** Twenty-four pages run from December 1 to Christmas Eve. If that is too many, see the shorter plans below.
2. **Choose pages for your child's age.** The Christmas set is split by age, so you can match the level. If you are not sure which fits, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).
3. **Print and number them.** Print everything in one batch and write a number on the back of each page.
4. **Hide them.** Put each page in an envelope or a numbered folder, and keep them where your child can see the pile but not open the next one.

## Each day

Open the day's page together and let your child color it whenever suits your day: after breakfast, before dinner, or in the quiet time before bed. When it is finished, hang it on a string or tape it to a wall. By the middle of December you will have a garland of your child's own work, and it shows how many days are left.

## Shorter plans

You do not need 24 pages.

- **Every other day.** Twelve pages from December 1 to Christmas Eve.
- **Weekdays only.** Color on school days, and leave weekends free for other plans.
- **The last twelve days.** Start on December 13 and count down to Christmas.
- **Repeat favorites.** Print a page your child loved twice, and let them color it in different colors.

## Ideas by age

**Ages 2–3.** Pick pages with big, simple shapes. A page is done in a minute or two, and that is fine. See [Big Shape coloring pages](/big-shape-coloring-pages/) for the biggest shapes.

**Ages 3–4.** Characters like Santa, a reindeer, or a stocking work well, with bold outlines and a few small parts.

**Ages 4–6.** Busier pages such as a gingerbread house, a Christmas tree, or a fireplace give children more to do and take a longer sitting.

**Mixed ages.** Print a stack for each child from different levels, and color at the same table. Each child opens their own envelope.

## Make it a little more special

- Ask a question with each page: "Who do you think lives in the gingerbread house?"
- Keep a small tradition, such as a cup of hot chocolate while you color.
- Color one page for someone else and give it as a card or a gift tag.
- Add a plain winter page for days when your child wants something different: [Winter coloring pages](/winter-coloring-pages/).

## For families who want the Nativity story

If your family would like pages with a Christian Christmas theme, the [Christian Christmas coloring pages](/christian-christmas-coloring-pages/) can be used the same way, one page a day, or mixed with the regular Christmas set.

## Starting late

You can start on any day. Count back from Christmas Eve, print as many pages as there are days left, and begin. Children do not mind starting a countdown at day 15.
