December is busy, and children are excited. A stack of printed [Christmas coloring pages](/christmas-coloring-pages/) is one of the easiest ways to give them something calm, creative, and useful to do.

Here are practical ideas that go beyond coloring a page and putting it in a drawer.

## 1. Color a garland

Color a few pages, then hang them on a string with clothespins across a wall or a window. Add a page every day, and by Christmas you have a garland made entirely by your child. Pages with a stocking or a Christmas tree look good from a distance, so they work especially well.

## 2. Make cards for family and friends

Fold a sheet of paper in half and tape or glue a colored page to the front. Your child can write a name inside, or you can write a short message while they dictate. Grandparents and relatives tend to keep these, and a handmade card costs almost nothing.

## 3. Create gift tags

Let your child color a page, then cut it into small rectangles and punch a hole in each. Write the name on the back and tie it to a present. A bold, simple picture such as a reindeer or Santa works best, because details disappear when the page is cut small.

## 4. Decorate a window

Tape finished pages to a window so the light shines through the paper. A wreath or a sleigh looks especially good there. When the day is over, take them down together and keep them in a folder for next year.

## 5. Color while you bake

When you are baking or wrapping presents, put a coloring page and crayons at the end of the table. A gingerbread house page is a natural fit for baking day. Your child stays close, stays busy, and still feels part of what you are doing.

## 6. Place mats for Christmas dinner

Print a page for each child, set it at their place with a cup of crayons, and let them color while the meal is being served. It keeps small hands busy, and it makes the children's seats feel special.

## 7. A gift for someone else

Let your child choose a page, color it, and give it to a neighbor, teacher, or relative. Giving something they made themselves is one of the nicest parts of the season for a small child.

## 8. Match the page to your child

A page that is too hard ends in frustration, and one that is too easy is finished in a minute. Christmas pages are split by age, so you can print different levels for children of different ages and let them color at the same table. For tips on choosing, see [how to choose coloring pages by age](/guides/coloring-pages-by-age/).

## Quick tips

- Print everything in one batch, with a few extra copies.
- Crayons are easy to use at a crowded table. If you use markers, choose washable ones.
- Keep a few pages in reserve for children who finish early.
- Save finished pages in a folder. Children enjoy looking through them the next December.
